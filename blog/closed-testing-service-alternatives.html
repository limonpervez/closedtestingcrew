Closed Testing Service Alternatives: How to Actually Evaluate One

Search for a closed testing service and you'll find a crowded field, cheap instant-tester services, "guaranteed 12 testers in an hour" listings, and a smaller number of services using real people. They are not the same product, even when the pricing looks similar. Here's how to tell them apart before you pay anyone, us included.

The one question that matters most

Ask directly: are the testers real people using real, previously-owned Android phones, or automated/emulated activity? Any legitimate service should answer this immediately and specifically. Vague answers ("our proprietary testing network," "advanced testing infrastructure") without a direct yes to "real phones, real people" are a signal to keep asking.

This matters because Google's systems are built to distinguish exactly this. Services running emulators, cloud device farms, or scripted opens put your entire developer account at risk, not just your current app. We cover the mechanics of what gets detected in do emulator testers get you banned.

Red flags in how a service is marketed

  • Unrealistically fast turnaround. The 14-day window is Google's own fixed requirement. No legitimate service can shorten it, so "12 testers in 24 hours" claims are about opt-ins, not the actual 14-day continuous cycle you need to pass.
  • No proof of work, ever. If a service won't show you any example of what tester activity or reporting looks like, you're being asked to trust a black box for two weeks.
  • Prices far below what real human time would cost. Genuinely recruiting, onboarding, and keeping 12-20 people actively engaged for 14 days takes real coordination effort. A price that only makes sense for scripted/automated activity is a signal about what you're actually buying.
  • No refund policy, or a refund policy with heavy conditions. A service confident in real, compliant testing can afford a straightforward money-back guarantee if Google doesn't approve. Extensive fine print around refunds is worth reading closely.

Good questions to ask any service before paying

  1. Are testers using their own personal, daily-driver phones, or dedicated test devices?
  2. Can I see any proof of daily activity during the cycle, not just a final report?
  3. What happens if Google rejects my Production access after the cycle, refund, free re-run, or nothing?
  4. Do testers actually use the app (navigate screens, generate real sessions), or just open and close it?
  5. Can I ask a tester a direct question or get feedback on a specific feature?

A service with nothing to hide will answer all five without hesitation. A bot-farm operation will dodge at least two or three.
